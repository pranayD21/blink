# Blink's live path on spoken calls (2026-11)

Task 38 runs written calls through the live path as speech, end to end. Each turn is read aloud, Flux transcribes it, the server's live reader reads it, the pill decides, the call ends into the after-call read, and every time along the way is recorded. **The voices are synthetic (macOS `say`); the words are real**, from the Halvorsen demo transcript and the five development calls. Each call ran once, live, on 2026-11-10. These are small samples: 77 heard turns in six calls.

## How it was run

`pnpm eval:spoken` (`scripts/speak-call.ts`, pure helpers in `src/server/eval/spoken.ts`) does the following for each call.

1. Parses the transcript with the app's own parser. It reads each turn aloud with `say`, in a voice per role: banker `Samantha`, expert `Daniel`, moderator `Karen`. `ffmpeg` converts each turn to 16 kHz mono 16-bit.
2. Lays the turns out one after another on two channels. The banker is on the microphone channel; the expert and the moderator are on the computer-audio channel, as on a real call. It leaves 1 s of silence at the start and 0.7 s between turns on different channels. Between two turns on the same channel it leaves 3 s, longer than Flux's end-of-turn wait, so they are heard as two turns. There are 4 s of silence at the end.
3. Starts the call with `POST /api/calls`. It takes a 30-second token for each channel from the call's own `stt-token` route and opens one Flux socket per channel, at the app's URL (`flux-general-en`, `eot_threshold=0.8`, `eot_timeout_ms=7000`; a test keeps the eval's copy equal to the app's). It then sends each channel in 80 ms frames at real-time pace, each frame once its audio has finished.
4. Builds turns with the app's pure assembler (`createTurnAssembler`) and posts them exactly as the banker's app does:
   - in order, one at a time, with a 3 s deadline;
   - on a timeout, a network error, 408, 429 or 5xx, the same seq, words and `Idempotency-Key` again;
   - an acknowledgement with `responseReceivedAt` and `shownAt` once the reply arrives;
   - `POST /end` once the sockets have closed and the queue is empty.
   No turn needed a retry in any of these runs. The eval does not reconnect a dropped Flux socket (the app does), and no socket dropped.
5. Waits for the after-call read, then compares its flags with the live ones in the server's database.

It imports the pure modules (`src/core/live/flux.ts`, `turns.ts`, `stats.ts`) and never the browser modules. It prints roles, pills, read statuses, times and turn labels, never a word of a transcript, and never a password or a token. Results (numbers and labels only) are in the git-ignored `data/spoken/results/`.

**Server.** It used a scratch database, `data/spoken.db`, prepared with `pnpm eval:spoken --prepare-db data/spoken.db`. That migrates, seeds the users, documents and the Halvorsen case at intake, activates the demo's manual policy, and replays the document-facts jobs from their recordings. `pnpm db:reset` was not used, because it always deletes `data/blink.db`, whatever `BLINK_DB_PATH` says. The server and its worker ran with `BLINK_DB_PATH=./data/spoken.db BLINK_LLM_MODE=live`, as `next dev -p 3217` plus `scripts/worker.ts`. The live reader was `claude-haiku-5-5` with its 2.5 s limit, and the after-call extraction was `claude-opus-5-5`. Nothing was recorded into `fixtures/llm-recordings/`.

**Sign-in.** The Halvorsen call ran in switcher mode with the `user_priya` cookie. The five development calls ran with the server in password mode and `--as user_priya`, which signs in through `/api/session/login` with her password from `BLINK_SEED_PASSWORDS`.

**Policies.**
- **Halvorsen:** the demo's manual policy (`manual-monitors-v1.json`), active and approved, as in the demo.
- **Development calls:** Oaktree. Its bundle compiled with 10 blocking `CLAUSE_DROPPED` coverage issues (Task 36), and the app refuses to approve such a policy. **For this eval only** it was installed into the scratch database the way `pnpm eval:real` does it: `installPolicies` in `src/server/eval/real.ts`, which calls `loadRealPolicy("oaktree", { allowCoverageGaps: true })`, then `createPolicy` and `finalizeCompile`. That leaves the version `COMPILE_FAILED`. It was never approved and never went through an app route. `pnpm eval:spoken --db data/spoken.db --prepare-case <callDir> --policy oaktree` creates Priya's case from the call's packet and points its `active_policy_version_id` at that version. `startCall` then pins the call to it.

**Times.**
- **End of speech to decision** is Task 20's measure: `shown_at` minus `speech_ended_at` from `call_turns`. The script also computed it on the client, and the two agreed on every turn of every call.
  - `speech_ended_at` is the end of Flux's audio window for the turn, on the call clock. **Flux's window ends a median 0.76 s after the speech really ended** (95th percentile 1.50 s, worst 1.66 s), so this measure leaves out most of Flux's end-of-turn wait.
- **End of audio to decision** is the stricter measure: from the true end of the synthetic speech (known exactly, because the eval placed it) to `shown_at`. `say` leaves under 50 ms of silence after a sentence.

### What the numbers say

- **Every turn that was not read live was an expert turn that hit the 2.5 s limit.** That is 22 of the 43 heard turns on the expert's channel (one of those 43 is the Halvorsen moderator's), and 0 of the 34 banker turns. Every read that finished took at most 2.40 s (typical 1.69 s). This is the `haiku-slow` verdict (Task 2) and Task 17's finding again, now on real words. Task 17 found the cause: a turn that carries a claim produces a long answer, and the 2.5 s limit cuts it off. Live red therefore came only from expert turns whose read finished within the limit (`chu` T3, `gupta-lau` T4 and T6, `gupta-horowitz` T4 and T18).
- **Speed target:** no turn was on screen within 1.0 s of the end of speech, and 36 of 77 were within 2.0 s. The target is 1.0 s typical and 2.0 s for 19 turns in 20. The 95th percentile is 2.81 s. By stage, over all 77 turns (typical · 95th percentile):
  - Flux, from the end of its window to `transcribed_at`: 0.06 · 0.16 s.
  - Waiting behind the previous turn's read, plus the network: 0.02 · 1.07 s.
  - The read: 1.88 · 2.51 s.
  - The rules: 0 · 0.002 s.
  - The reply to `shown_at`: 0.003 · 0.007 s.

  **The read is the stage that takes the time.** The two turns over 3 s (`gupta-lau` T12, 4.18 s; `gupta-horowitz` T5, 3.36 s) were banker turns that waited for the expert turn before them to be read, because a call's turns are read one at a time. Measured from the true end of the audio, Flux's own end-of-turn wait adds a median 0.87 s (95th percentile 1.55 s) before `transcribed_at`.
- **Live flags against the after-call read:** they matched on no call. Mostly the after-call read flags more. It reads the whole call with no time limit, so it can flag turns the live reader ran out of time on; all six labelled turns are among them. The live path flagged two turns the after-call read did not: `chu` T3 and `gupta-horowitz` T18. The app keeps both hidden anyway: every live flag becomes a `live` finding for compliance unless an after-call policy finding covers it (Task 13). On `chu`, which Task 37 found comes back with nothing flagged, the live reader alone flagged the call.

## Transcription on real audio: Earnings-21

Two files of Earnings-21's ten-hour evaluation subset (`eval10-file-metadata.csv`), from `https://github.com/revdotcom/speech-datasets` (`earnings21/`). The transcripts and tags are licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0.
- `4387332`: Zagg Inc, third-quarter 2020 call, 21.8 min, a 24 kHz MP3.
- `4366522`: Aldeyra Therapeutics Inc, second-quarter call, 30.3 min, a 22.05 kHz MP3.

They are the two shortest files in the subset, which keeps the download small. Media, references (`transcripts/nlp_references/<id>.nlp`) and entity tags (`transcripts/wer_tags/<id>.wer_tag.json`) were downloaded into the git-ignored `data/spoken/earnings21/`.

**How.** `pnpm eval:spoken --earnings21 4387332,4366522 --minutes 10` cuts the first 600 s of each file with `ffmpeg` to 16 kHz mono, then adds 4 s of silence. It streams each file to Flux at real-time pace, with the app's URL and the same 80 ms frames, both files at once on two sockets. The hypothesis is every `EndOfTurn` transcript, in order.
- **Normaliser** (`normalizeWords`): lowercase. Hyphens, dashes and slashes become spaces. Every other character that is not a letter, a digit or a space is deleted, so "$5.2" becomes "52" and "It's" becomes "its". Then split on spaces. Nothing is expanded: "21" and "twenty one" are different words. The reference's tokens are normalised the same way; a token that splits ("10-K") keeps its tags on each part.
- **Which part of the reference.** The reference has no word times, so the ten minutes are matched to the reference prefix that best fits them. The alignment is Levenshtein (each substitution, deletion and insertion costs 1) with the end of the reference left free; among equally good prefixes the longest is used (`alignPrefix`). Word error rate is (substitutions + deletions + insertions) / reference words in that prefix.
- **Numbers correct:** the share of reference words containing a digit, after normalising, whose aligned hypothesis word is the same word.
- **Company names correct:** the same, for reference words the dataset tags `ORG` (through `wer_tags` and the file's `wer_tag.json`). The dataset's tags are used, not capitalisation.

| File | Reference words matched | Word error rate | Substitutions · deletions · insertions | Numbers correct | Company names correct |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `4387332` (Zagg) | 1,820 | 11.5% | 104 · 62 · 44 | 8 of 35 (23%) | 28 of 32 (88%) |
| `4366522` (Aldeyra) | 1,338 | 12.5% | 65 · 41 · 61 | 0 of 36 (0%) | 23 of 28 (82%) |
| **Both** | **3,158** | **11.9%** | **169 · 103 · 105** | **8 of 71 (11%)** | **51 of 60 (85%)** |

- **Numbers.** Flux writes most numbers as words ("twenty twenty", "ten", "nineteen"). Under this normaliser, which expands nothing, a number written in words never matches the reference's digits. All 8 matches are alphanumeric codes ("Q2", "Q3", "Q4", "S20"). Among the aligned pairs, numbers said as one word mostly came out as that word ("10" as "ten", "12" as "twelve", "19" as "nineteen", "30" as "thirty"). Numbers said as several words ("2020", "2191", "$115") cannot be judged token by token. So the 11% is mostly a measure of how Flux writes numbers, not of whether it heard them. It does matter for Blink: the live reader and the after-call read receive numbers as words.
- **Company names.** The misses are mostly the company's own name: "Zagg" as "zag", "Aldeyra" as "alderes", "aldaris", "aldera" and "aldero", and "InvisibleShield" split into two words. An issuer's name is what a case is about. A reader that matches on the exact name would miss these, so the live and after-call readers must tolerate near spellings.

## Limits

- Synthetic voices and digital silence: there is no room noise, no crosstalk, no accent beyond the three voices, and no telephone codec. Flux's word error rate on the six synthetic calls was 9.5% over 1,274 written words: halvorsen 6.1%, chu 4.4%, gupta-lau 14.8%, gupta-horowitz 8.9%, kyprios-1 2.4%, kyprios-2 0%. That is the heard turns joined and compared with the written turns joined, with the normaliser described under Earnings-21. It includes the transcripts' fillers ("ah", "um") and editorial marks ("[UI]", "(sic)"), which `say` reads and Flux may drop. Real calls will transcribe worse (the Earnings-21 figures above are the better guide to that) and may end turns differently.
- The turn gaps are fixed (0.7 s between speakers, 3 s on the same channel). Real conversation overlaps, and turns arrive faster.
- One run per call. The live reader's timeouts are consistent with Task 17, but which short expert turns finish in time can change from run to run.
- The development calls were judged under Oaktree loaded despite its coverage gaps, which the app would never activate. The demo policy is the only approved policy here.
